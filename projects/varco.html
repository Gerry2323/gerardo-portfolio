LUNAR MATERIALS RESEARCH

VARCO Lunar Coating Research

Vacuum Regolith Composite Coatings for Lunar Infrastructure

VARCO investigates lunar-regolith and ceramic composite coatings that could protect equipment and infrastructure operating on the Moon.

Project Overview

The VARCO project explores how lunar regolith can be combined with ceramic materials and deposited as a protective coating. This approach supports in-situ resource utilization by using material available on the Moon instead of transporting all protective materials from Earth.

The research focuses on developing and testing coating compositions, manufacturing processes, substrates, and characterization methods for future lunar applications.

The Engineering Problem

Lunar equipment must operate in an environment containing abrasive regolith, extreme temperature changes, vacuum, radiation, and limited access to replacement materials.

Transporting construction and repair material from Earth is expensive. VARCO examines whether processed lunar material could become part of a locally manufactured protective coating.

Research Objectives

  • Develop regolith and ceramic composite feedstock mixtures.
  • Deposit experimental coatings using thermal-spray manufacturing processes.
  • Compare different material compositions and spray conditions.
  • Evaluate coating adhesion, hardness, microstructure, and surface quality.
  • Investigate processes suitable for future lunar-surface manufacturing.

Phase I: Vacuum Plasma Spray Testing

During the first testing campaign, the team used vacuum plasma spray to deposit lunar-regolith and alumina-based coatings. Initial aluminum coupons experienced delamination, so the team transitioned to mild-steel substrates.

The campaign produced 128 coated coupons and provided important information about substrate compatibility, ceramic overheating, equipment limitations, cooling requirements, and feedstock behavior.

Material Compositions

The experimental coating matrix included mixtures ranging from pure alumina to pure lunar-regolith simulant.

  • 100% alumina
  • 75% alumina and 25% regolith
  • 50% alumina and 50% regolith
  • 25% alumina and 75% regolith
  • 100% lunar-regolith simulant

Proposed Lunar Manufacturing Process

  1. Excavate lunar regolith.
  2. Sieve and classify the material by particle size.
  3. Load the processed material into an ISRU feed system.
  4. Deposit the protective coating onto the structure.
  5. Inspect and characterize the finished coating.

Testing and Characterization

Planned testing is intended to compare coating quality and determine which material compositions and manufacturing parameters provide the best performance.

  • Vickers or Knoop hardness testing
  • Coating-thickness measurement
  • Adhesion evaluation
  • Microscopy and microstructure analysis
  • Surface and crack inspection
  • Comparison between coating compositions

Future Work

The next research phase includes investigating cold spray and other lower-temperature deposition methods. These processes may reduce thermal damage and provide a more practical option for rover-mounted or lunar-surface coating systems.

Additional work will include optimizing feedstock preparation, substrate treatment, spray distance, process parameters, and mechanical testing.
